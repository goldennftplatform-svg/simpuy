The whole game in four seasons

  1. 1

    Spring — claim & plant

    Pick a region (real hop country: WA · ID · OR). Plant hop yards on open dirt. In dry country, plant next to creeks or plan on wells. Everything you plant pays this fall.

  2. 2

    Summer — one crew order, choose well

    Train the bines (+10% vigor), preventative spray (disasters half as likely and half as cruel), or hire extra hands (+3 acres picked this fall). Watch the Projected fall number in the HUD — it updates as you build. Keep every yard watered.

  3. 3

    Fall — pick, dry, sell

    Harvest is automatic. Kilns dry your crop (7,000 lb each) — kilns are mandatory: only 2,000 lb per year can sell wet, the rest spoils. Cabins bring pickers (6 acres each). Contracts pay first, the rest sells at spot price.

  4. 4

    Winter — count & scheme

    Pay upkeep, answer the Clarion's almanac quiz ($25 purse), sign next year's contracts at fixed prices — or gamble on the spot market.

How to actually win

  • Water is everything in dry country. One well serves 9 tiles — cluster yards around it like a donut.
  • Kilns are not optional. Each dries 7,000 lb; only 2,000 lb a year sells wet. Every acre past your kiln line is fertilizer. Match kilns to yards (~1 per 6-7 mature acres).
  • Cabins pick 6 acres. Unpicked acres lose 35% of their crop. Count cabins before you count yards.
  • Contracts = safety, spot = gamble. Check the market sparkline in the broker's office — sign when the trend is falling, ride spot when it's climbing. Big contracts can make rivals poach your buyers.
  • Reputation is money. Win the quiz, help neighbors, impress Meeker — buyers pay more for a good name.
  • First-year bines yield 68%. Plant in spring anyway; they pay full from next year.

Hit $32,000 net worth to be crowned Hop King of the World. Drop under -$400 in winter and the bank takes the farm. Rivals pass $34,000 and steal the crown — pass them to take it back.

HOPCITY

Questions from the county clerk

How do I win?

Reach $32,000 net worth (cash + buildings). Net worth updates in the HUD every turn. Yards count $80 each, kilns $300, wells $120, cabins $160, HQ $400 — so buildings count even before they pay off.

How do I lose?

If cash sits below -$400 when winter books close, the bank forecloses. Upkeep hits every winter, so keep a cushion. There is exactly one save — no reloads.

A rival was crowned Hop King. Is it over?

No. The first fortune past $34,000 wears the crown — pass their worth and the crown is yours. The game only truly ends at your win or foreclosure.

My yards keep dying in summer. Why?

In dry regions (rain under 45%) yards lose big vigor unless watered. A yard is watered if a creek or well touches any of its 8 neighboring tiles, or if you pay the summer "haul water" job. Thirsty yards show a red dot; watered show blue.

What do the tile colors mean?

Green = open dirt (build here). Blue waves = creek (free water adjacency). Gray = bedrock, dark green = timber — both need Clear Land first. The gold-outlined center tile is your HQ. Yard health shows as an inset ring: gold = strong, red = failing. First-year yards look slightly faded.

Why did my harvest earn so little?

Three usual culprits: not enough kilns (only 2,000 lb per year can sell wet at ~18% value — everything else with no kiln room spoils outright), not enough cabins (unpicked acres lose 35% of their crop), or hauling fees (4-6% of gross by region, more during rail disputes). The harvest modal shows each deduction.

Should I sign winter contracts?

Contracts lock a fixed price for a set amount of next year's crop — insurance against a bust. If you sign more pounds than you harvest, you breach: spot price takes an 8% penalty next fall. Sign conservatively until you know your yield.

What's the market price based on?

A base price that drifts each winter, multiplied by boom/bust events, your region's price rating, your average crop quality (vigor), and your reputation. Watch the arrow in the HUD.

What does reputation do?

Good rep (winning the almanac quiz, impressing Meeker, forgiving a neighbor's debt) adds a premium to every pound you sell. Bad rep (breaching contracts, squeezing neighbors) cuts it. Rep fades slowly — keep earning it.

What's the winter quiz?

The Valley Clarion prints one almanac question each winter. Answer right for a $25 purse and a small reputation bump. All questions come from the real history of Ezra Meeker and the Northwest hop boom.

Does the game save?

Automatically, in this browser, after every action. No account, no server. "Stake a new claim" plows the old save under permanently.

Any keyboard shortcuts?

Space advances the season from the farm screen. That's the only one — everything else is clicks, like 1883 intended.
